Frame the problem

This is the receptionist. I'm going to call the clinic now — you'll hear exactly what a patient hears.

Run three exchanges, in this order. Each one proves a different capability, and the third is the one that earns trust.

1 · Book an appointment

"Hi, I'd like to book an appointment with Dr. Chen next week."

Let it ask for your name and preferred time. Give a real name and a slot. It checks live availability, holds the slot, and confirms. Remember the name you use — you'll point at it on screen in ninety seconds.

2 · Ask about the clinic

"What are your opening hours, and do you have parking?"

Answers from the clinic's own profile and knowledge base — not from a general model. Worth saying out loud: this never guesses about your clinic.

3 · Ask something it can't answer

"I've had chest pain since this morning — what should I take for it?"

That's the most important thing it just did. It refused. It doesn't give medical advice, and when it doesn't know, it offers a human instead of inventing an answer.

Why

The retrieval layer returns a no-answer signal rather than a low-confidence guess, and the agent turns that into a transfer offer. Clinical buyers care about this more than anything else in the demo — give it room, don't rush past it.

If it stumbles

Speech recognition can mishear a name or date. Don't fight it on screen — say "let me repeat that, same as a patient would" and say it again. Recovering naturally reads as robustness. Re-running the whole session reads as fragility.

04

Trust and control

3 min
Security
/app/security

Every time anyone — a staff member or the AI — opens a patient record, it's logged. Who, what, when, and whether it was a human or the agent.

Show the audit log and its filters. Point out that entries record which fields changed, never the values — the log is evidence, not a second copy of the patient record.

Agent Settings
/app/agent

Greeting, voice, language, and the clinic's own rules. Show that the clinic controls what the AI says — it isn't a black box they have to accept as shipped.

Clinic Profile & Team
/app/clinic · /app/team

Hours, services, doctors — the facts the AI answers from. Then Team: invite staff, admin versus medical staff roles. Reads stay open to staff; anything that changes what the AI says to patients is admin-only.

05

It's a platform, not an install

3 min
Platform console
/admin

Everything so far was one clinic's view. This is the operator's view — and it's the same system serving every clinic on it.

Walk: Organizations → Plans → Subscriptions → Usage → Phone Numbers. One worker serves many clinics; a call is routed to the right clinic by the number that was dialled.

Close on Audit Log — the platform keeps its own record, separate from each clinic's.

Close

End here, not on the dashboard. Act 5 is what turns "a nice tool for one clinic" into "a business that scales to a hundred" — and it's the answer to the question they were probably already forming.

!

Don't touch these

Billing

Don't click Upgrade. POST /billing/upgrade returns 501 by design — it used to grant Enterprise with no payment, and it was deliberately disabled rather than left live. Show the plan page, describe the tiers, don't press the button.

Agent Settings

Don't demo escalation rules. The routing-rules surface reads from a table nothing writes to, so changes there have no effect on a live call. Stay on greeting, voice and language, which are wired end to end.

Security

Don't claim the encryption values are measured. AES-256 and TLS 1.3 are stated intended configuration on that page — nothing verifies TDE is on or which TLS version terminated the request. If asked, say it's the deployment standard, not a live check. Over-claiming compliance to a clinical buyer is the one mistake that's expensive later.

Phone

Don't offer to dial the number. Inbound routing is stuck on the carrier side and no call currently reaches the app. If they ask, the honest and entirely normal answer is below in Q&A.

?

Questions they will ask

Can we call the number right now?

"Number provisioning is with the carrier — the DID routing is being switched over this week. Everything downstream of it is what you just watched." True, unembarrassing, and moves on. Don't improvise a live attempt.

What happens if it gets something wrong?

Three answers, in order: it declines rather than guesses (they saw it); every call is scored and flagged for review; and a human can edit or cancel anything it did. Show the Quality tab again if they press.

Where does patient data go?

The knowledge base embeddings run locally — clinic documents and patient questions never leave the machine. Data is stored per-clinic with every query scoped to that clinic. Every record access is audited.

Does it work with our existing system?

Show /app/ehr. Be straight: integration is configured per EHR, and a booking always succeeds locally even if the EHR is down — it syncs after, and the status is visible. Never promise a specific vendor on the spot.

What languages?

The voice stack is configured for English, Hindi and Telugu. Demo in English — you haven't rehearsed the others and accent handling varies.

How many clinics can it handle?

Act 5 is your answer — one worker, many clinics, routed by dialled number. If pushed on scale, say horizontal scaling is the next infrastructure step rather than claiming a number you'd have to defend.
