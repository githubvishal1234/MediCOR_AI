MediCOR Screen Reference

Every screen in the product, what it does, and the order a real user meets them. The flow starts with a phone call — the two consoles exist to read and steer what that call produced.

What this is for

Every call a clinic misses is a patient who books somewhere else.

Reception is on another line, at lunch, or gone at six — and the phone keeps ringing. MediCOR AI answers all of it. It picks up in the clinic's own voice, books against real availability, answers what callers actually ask about, and hands over to a human the moment it is out of its depth.

Everything it does lands in the clinic's dashboard as it happens, so staff arrive to a record rather than a voicemail queue. It is sold as software, not an installation — one system serves many clinics, and onboarding a new one is a row in a table.

On the phone

What the caller gets

  • Answers every call, day or night, with no hold queue
  • Books appointments against live doctor availability and confirms on the call
  • Answers clinic questions — hours, services, fees, parking, insurance
  • Declines rather than guesses. No medical advice; offers a human instead
  • Configured for English, Hindi and Telugu

In the dashboard

What the clinic gets

  • Full transcripts with an AI summary, sentiment and outcome for every call
  • Quality scoring — calls judged automatically, with a review queue
  • Appointments and patient records staff can edit, including anything the AI did
  • Live view of calls in progress, pushed as they happen
  • Analytics in the clinic's own timezone — volume, resolution, handle time

Trust

What makes it safe to buy

  • Full audit trail — every record access logged, field names never values
  • Roles — staff read; only admins change what the AI says to patients
  • Documents never leave the machine — knowledge indexing runs locally
  • Bookings survive outages. A booking succeeds even if the EHR is down, then syncs
  • Each clinic's data is sealed from every other clinic

As a platform

What makes it a business

  • One worker serves every clinic, routed by the number that was dialled
  • Plans and limits enforced live, per clinic
  • Operator console for organisations, subscriptions, usage and numbers
  • Impersonation for support, recorded against both parties
  • Usage computed from call records, so billing cannot drift

Where the flow starts

There are three front doors, and only one of them is the product. A patient dials the clinic and the voice agent answers — no screen involved. Everything the agent does is written through the same API the dashboard reads, so by the time staff open the app, the call is already a row.

For a demo, that ordering matters: start with the call, then open the dashboard. Showing the screens first makes them look like data entry. Showing them after a call makes them look like the consequence of one.

Security & Privacy
/app/security

The clinic's own compliance surface — the page they would show an auditor.

  • Three sections: encryption, access control, and the audit trail
  • Every record access is logged with who, what, when, and whether it was a human or the AI
  • Entries record which fields changed, never the values — the log is evidence, not a second copy of the patient record

Don't over-claim

The AES-256 and TLS 1.3 values are stated intended configuration. Nothing on this page measures them. Describe them as the deployment standard, never as a verified live check.

Platform console

10 screens · /admin

The operator's view across every clinic on the platform — you, the vendor, not the customer. This is the part that makes it a business rather than an installation, and it is worth closing a demo on.

Who a super admin is

Not a clinic account with extra permissions — a separate account type in its own table, with its own login, its own 8-hour session, and a token the clinic API refuses outright. The three kinds of session in this system (staff, super admin, voice agent) each reject the other two, so there is no privilege ladder to climb from a clinic login to platform control. There is also no sign-up page: the first operator account is created from the command line, on purpose.

Platform overview
/admin

The operator's morning screen — is the platform healthy, and did anything change overnight.

  • Totals across every clinic: organisations, how many are suspended, voice minutes used against the limit
  • A recent-activity feed mixing new clinics, admin actions and call activity, so a suspension or a plan change is visible without hunting for it
Organizations
/admin/organizations

Every clinic registered on the platform, in one table.

  • Columns: organisation, plan, users, patients, voice minutes, status
  • Search clinics by name; filter to active or suspended
  • This is the customer list — in a demo, say out loud that each row is a separate tenant whose data no other row can reach
Organization detail
/admin/organizations/:id the account screen

Everything about one clinic, and the controls that act on it. The busiest screen in the console.

  • Contact information, the admin or owner, and the full user list with roles
  • Plan and usage together: subscription state, voice minute limit, and counts of calls, appointments and patients
  • Suspend the clinic — and the reason you type is recorded in the audit log, not just the action
  • Impersonate the clinic to reproduce a support problem in their own dashboard
Two things to say

Suspension cuts off in-progress calls, not just sign-ins — a suspended clinic's phone AI stops answering immediately. And impersonation is not invisible: entries written while impersonating name both the operator and the clinic user, so support access is never mistaken for the clinic's own activity.

Users
/admin/users

Every user account on the platform, flattened across all clinics.

  • Name, email, which clinic, role, status, created date and last activity
  • Filter by role — admin, medical staff, or AI agent, which is a real account type: the voice agent acts as a user, so its actions attribute to it rather than to a person
  • Search by name or email across every clinic at once, which is how a support request usually starts
Plans
/admin/plans product definition

Where the commercial product is actually defined — not a marketing page, the live entitlement rules.

  • Per plan: name, description, monthly price, user limit, patient limit, voice minutes per month, and knowledge base access
  • Limits left blank mean unlimited
  • Each plan shows how many clinics are on it, and can be retired without deleting it
The interesting part

These flags are enforced live, and how they refuse depends on who is asking. A staff member gets a clear message naming their plan and what to do. Anything the voice agent touches degrades silently instead — it gives the same ordinary "I'll put you through to someone" reply a missing answer produces. A caller must never hear about a billing problem, so a plan gate can never become a failed call.

Subscriptions
/admin/subscriptions

Which clinic is on which plan, and in what commercial state.

  • Clinic, plan, status — active, past due or cancelled — voice minutes, and when it last changed
  • Searchable by clinic or plan
Deliberate

A past due clinic keeps full access. Cutting off a clinic's phone line over a late invoice is a collections decision, not an entitlement one, and it would take patients down with it. Only an explicitly cancelled subscription loses features.

Usage management
/admin/usage

What each clinic is actually consuming — the basis for invoicing and for upgrade conversations.

  • Usage by clinic: calls, appointments, patients, voice minutes and knowledge base use, from a chosen start date
  • All-time plan usage alongside it, so you can see who is pressed against a limit and who has headroom
  • Clinics with no limit set are shown as such rather than as zero
Why it's trustworthy

Voice minutes are computed from the call records each time, not incremented into a counter. A counter drifts the moment one update is missed, and the clinic's bill then disagrees with the operator's report. Billing periods are calendar months in the clinic's timezone, not the server's.

Phone Numbers
/admin/phone-numbers multi-tenant routing

The routing table that makes one system serve many clinics. Technically the most important screen in the console.

  • Assign a number to a clinic, with a label such as "Main reception" and the telephony trunk it arrives on
  • Numbers are globally unique — one number belongs to exactly one clinic, and that is enforced, not conventional
How a call finds its clinic

One agent worker serves every clinic. When a call arrives the agent reads the number that was dialled, looks it up here, and gets back that clinic's identity, timezone, greeting and voice — then handles the call as that clinic. Clinics are not separate deployments, and onboarding one is adding a row here rather than standing up a server.

Support
/admin/support

The other end of the clinic's support screen — the operator's inbox.

  • Requests from every clinic, filterable by clinic and by status
  • Pairs with impersonation on the organisation screen: read the request, then go and see what they saw
Audit logs
/admin/audit-log

The platform's own record of what operators did — the accountability layer over this whole console.

  • Timestamp, which super admin, the action, the clinic affected, any target user, the resource touched, and success or failure
  • Filter by action, clinic, status or date; search across everything; platform-wide entries are marked as such
  • Each entry keeps its own ID and the originating user agent, so a specific action can be cited later
Two logs, not one

This is not the same log as the clinic's Security page. This one records cross-clinic operator actions — suspensions, plan changes, impersonations — and clinics cannot see it. Theirs records access to their own patient data, and is the page they would show an auditor. Failed actions are recorded here too, which is the point: an attempt that did not succeed is exactly what an investigation needs to see.
